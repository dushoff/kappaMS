\begin{abstract}
	Heterogeneity is a hallmark of infectious disease spread. It can arise from differences among individuals, their locations and their mixing patterns. But some heterogeneity emerges from the disease dynamics themselves.
	While heterogeneity arising from individual variation and mixing patterns, especially in the invasion phase of epidemics has been studied, heterogeneity arising from disease dynamics remain unexplored.

Here we explore variation in the case-reproductive number Rc (number of cases caused by a given infector). Even in simple compartmental models, Rc varies through time over the course of an outbreak, and it also varies due to random individual differences in infectious duration (and in random contacts for discrete-individual models). For a class of compartmental models that includes the discrete and mean-field versions of the canonical SIR model, we show that the variation in Rc over the course of an epidemic is independent of model parameters. 

We consider the contribution to variation in Rc from cohorts of simultaneously-infected infectors. We found that as the basic reproduction number R0 increases from 1, between-cohort variability appears and increases, driven by change in the effective reproduction number over time. However, susceptible depletion over individual infection durations reduces within-cohort variability, maintaining constant total variance in cases per case. We proved that, over entire SIR epidemics seeded by one infector in an entirely susceptible population, this conservation of variance is precise and independent of R0. We apply the law of total variance to provide coherence between mean-field and discrete-individual models, as the stochastic (Poisson) contribution to variance is always equal to the mean of the mixing distribution. 

Despite constant variation in Rc over an entire epidemic, we show how that variation evolves dynamically and differently depending on model parameters. This complexity presents a challenge for inferring parametric individual-level heterogeneity from case data, as the dynamics of the susceptible pool affects observed individual-level heterogeneity. We highlight this epidemiological challenge by generating case data from the early phase of simulated epidemics.
