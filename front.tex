\begin{abstract}
	Heterogeneity is a hallmark of infectious disease spread. It can arise from differences among individuals, their locations and their mixing patterns. This heterogeneity has consequences for disease dynamics, i.e., in the context of superspreading, where a large number of infections caused by a single infector can lead to an epidemic, despite a subcritical $\mathcal{R}_0$.
	As a result, these forms of heterogeneity and their consequences, especially in the invasion phase of epidemics, have been studied extensively. However, heterogeneity also arises from disease dynamics, wherein changes in the population of individuals with different infection statuses over the course of the epidemic, can lead to changes in the realized outcomes from underlying intrinsic variations.
