\noindent\textbf{Box 1. Linking variance.} 

During the early stages of an outbreak, secondary-cases counts are typically modeled using a negative-binomial distribution, with  mean $\mu$ and  shape, or “dispersion,” parameter $k$. 
This distribution is linked through Poissonification to a Gamma distribution with the same mean and shape parameter values.
Indeed, the Gamma distribution describes the variation among individuals in expected infectiousness.


The statistic $k^{-1}$ increases with variability around the mean in both distributions. In particular,
for the negative binomial distribution, $k^{-1}$ is equal to the excess variance with respect to the Poisson distribution with mean $\mu$, divided by $\mu^2$.
For the Gamma distribution, $k^{-1}$ equals the squared coefficient of variation.
As $k^{-1}$ approaches zero, the negative binomial distribution converges to the Poisson distribution with  mean $\mu$, while Gamma distribution converges to a point mass at $\mu$.

We therefore advocate using $\kappa = k^{-1}$,  as a (generalized) \emph{dispersion} parameter, with larger values indicating greater heterogeneity in expected infectiousness and secondary cases.
