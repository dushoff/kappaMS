\noindent\textbf{Box 1. Linking variance.} 

Individual-level activity is typically estimated using a negative-binomial distribution, with  mean $\mu$ and  shape, or “dispersion” parameter $k$.
This distribution can be represented as a Poisson mixture whose mixing distribution is Gamma  with the same mean and shape parameter values.
 The  variance of the negative binomial and Gamma distributions are $\mu + \mu^2/k$ and $mu^2/k$, respectively.
Thus, both  distributions can be parametrized  by their mean $\mu$ and 
a (generalized) \emph{dispersion} parameter, $\kappa$, defined as $k^{-1}$.

For the Gamma mixing distribution, $\kappa$ equals the squared coefficient of variation.
For the negative binomial distribution, $\kappa$ is equal to the excess variance with respect to the Poisson distribution with mean $\mu$, divided by $\mu^2$.
The statistic $\kappa$ increases with variability around the mean.
In particular,
as $\kappa$ approaches zero, Gamma distribution converges to a point mass at $\mu$, and the negative binomial distribution converges to the Poisson distribution with  mean $\mu$.

We, therefore, use $\kappa$, throughout this paper, to characterize dispersion in both secondary cases and expected infectiousness.
