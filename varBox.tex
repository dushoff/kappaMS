\noindent\textbf{Box 1. Linking variance.} 

Individual-level activity is typically estimated using a negative-binomial distribution, with a shape, or “dispersion” parameter $k$.
This distribution is implicitly or explicitly linked 
The mean and variance of a mixed Poisson distribution whose mixing distribution has mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$ are, respectively, $\mu$ and  $\mu+\sigma^2$.
Thus, both mixing and mixed Poisson distributions can be described using  two quantities: mean $\mu$ and 
(generalized) \emph{dispersion} parameter, $\kappa$, defined as $\sigma^2/\mu^2$.

The statistic $\kappa$ increases with variability around the mean and equals zero for a Dirac delta function, making it a desirable measure of dispersion.

The secondary-case distribution is a mixed Poisson distribution, with expected infectiousness as the mixing distribution. 
%Here, the continuous mixing distribution describes expected infectiousness, and the mixed Poisson distribution represents the secondary-case, resulting from the stochastic realization (Poissonification) of expected infectiousness.
Hence, we use a single parameter, $\kappa$, to characterize dispersion in both secondary cases and expected infectiousness throughout this paper.
