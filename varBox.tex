\noindent\textbf{Box 1. Linking variance.} 

During the early stages of an outbreak, secondary-cases counts are typically modeled using a negative-binomial distribution, with mean $\mu$ and shape, or “dispersion,” parameter $k$. 
This distribution is linked through Poissonification to a Gamma distribution with the same mean and shape parameter values.
Here, the Gamma distribution describes variation among individuals in expected infectiousness.

We note a couple of points. First, the squared coefficient of variation (SCV) of the underlying Gamma distribution is $\kappa=1/k$, so its variance is $\sigmaAct^2 = \kappa \mu^2$. The resulting variance in secondary-case counts is $\sigma^2 = \kappa\mu^2 + \mu$, with the last term arising from the Poisson step of the negative-binomial construction.

Second, the relationship between $\sigmaAct^2$ and $\sigma^2$ holds regardless of the form of the expected infectiousness distribution.

As $\kappa$ approaches zero, the distribution of expected infectiousness converges to a point mass at $\mu$, while the corresponding distribution of secondary-case counts converges to a Poission distribution with mean $\mu$.
We therefore advocate using $\kappa$,  as a (generalized) \emph{dispersion} parameter, with larger values indicating greater heterogeneity in expected infectiousness and secondary cases.
